Passwords are the first line of defence for your hosting account, email, control panel, and any applications running on your server. A weak password can be cracked in seconds by automated tools, while a strong one can take thousands of years. Despite this, many people still use predictable, easy-to-guess passwords — and it only takes one compromised account to lose your website, your data, and your customers' trust. This guide explains how to create strong passwords, what to avoid, and how to manage them safely.

---

## What makes a password strong?

A strong password is one that is difficult for both humans and computers to guess. The most important factor is **length** — the longer a password, the exponentially harder it is to crack using brute-force methods.

### Core requirements

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Length" icon="ruler-horizontal">
    At least **12 characters**, preferably **16 or more**. Every additional
    character dramatically increases the time required to crack the password.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Complexity" icon="shuffle">
    Use a mixture of **uppercase letters**, **lowercase letters**, **numbers**,
    and **punctuation marks** or symbols.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Unpredictability" icon="dice">
    The best password is one that appears completely random to anyone who is not
    you. Avoid patterns, keyboard sequences, or anything predictable.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Uniqueness" icon="key">
    Use a **different password for every account**. Reusing passwords means that
    if one service is breached, every account sharing that password is at risk.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Techniques for creating strong passwords

Here are several practical methods for generating passwords that are both strong and memorable:

- **Passphrase method:** Choose four or more unrelated words and combine them. For example: `correct-horse-battery-staple-9`. This is long, random, and easy to remember.
- **Abbreviation method:** Take an uncommon phrase and use the first letter of each word, mixing in numbers and symbols. For example, _"You can't always get what you want"_ becomes `yCag5wyw#`.
- **Deliberate misspelling:** Introduce intentional misspellings and substitute symbols — but avoid obvious substitutions like `@` for `a` (attackers know these patterns).
- **Place a symbol in the middle of a word:** For example, `vege%tarian` — this disrupts dictionary-based attacks without making the password hard to remember.
- **Combine multiple techniques** for the strongest result.

<Tip>
  The most secure approach is to use a **password manager** (see below) to
  generate and store fully random passwords. You only need to remember one
  strong master password to unlock the manager.
</Tip>

## Common password mistakes to avoid

Many passwords that seem clever are actually well-known to attackers and their cracking tools. Avoid the following:

<Warning>
  The techniques below are frequently used in dictionary and rule-based attacks.
  Even if your password feels unique to you, if it follows one of these
  patterns, it may be cracked quickly.
</Warning>

- **Dictionary words** — any single word found in any language dictionary
- **Your username, real name, or any other name** — attackers try these first
- **Names from pop culture** — movies, TV shows, sports teams, fictional characters
- **Keyboard patterns** — `qwerty`, `asdfgh`, `nbvcx`, `123456`
- **Simple character substitutions** — `p@ssw0rd`, `sup3rs3cr3t`. Cracking programs know every common substitution.
- **Adding a single character to the end** — `password1`, `sunshine!`
- **Capitalising just the first letter** — `Password` is barely stronger than `password`
- **Reversing, doubling, or mirroring a simple word** — `tac`, `catcat`, `cattac`
- **Dates, phone numbers, or postcodes** — all easily guessable with minimal information about you

## Using a password manager

A password manager generates, stores, and autofills long, random, unique passwords for every account you have. You only need to remember a single strong master password — the manager handles everything else.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Bitwarden" icon="shield" href="https://bitwarden.com">
    Free and open-source. Available on all platforms and browsers. Recommended
    for individuals and small teams.
  </Card>
  <Card title="1Password" icon="lock" href="https://1password.com">
    Premium password manager popular with businesses. Supports team vaults and
    has strong security credentials.
  </Card>
  <Card title="KeePass" icon="database" href="https://keepass.info">
    Free, open-source, and stores your vault locally rather than in the cloud.
    Good choice if you prefer not to use a cloud-based service.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dashlane" icon="key" href="https://www.dashlane.com">
    User-friendly password manager with a built-in VPN and dark web monitoring
    on premium plans.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  Enable **two-factor authentication (2FA)** on your password manager account
  for an additional layer of protection. Even if someone discovers your master
  password, they cannot access your vault without the second factor.
</Tip>

## Safeguarding your passwords

Creating a strong password is only half the battle — you also need to keep it safe.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Never share your password with anyone">
    Not with support staff, colleagues, or family members. Netstreet Africa
    support staff will **never** ask for your password. If anyone claiming to be
    from Netstreet Africa asks for your password, it is a scam — see our
    [Known Scams](/security/known-scams) page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Never write your password down">
    A password written on a sticky note, in a notebook, or in a plain text file
    on your desktop is far less secure than one in a password manager.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change passwords after a suspected breach">
    If you receive a data breach notification for any service you use, change
    your password for that service immediately — and change the password of any
    other account that shared the same password.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable two-factor authentication (2FA)">
    Where available, enable 2FA on your Netstreet Africa client area account,
    cPanel, and any other services you use. 2FA means that even a correct
    password alone is not enough to gain access.
  </Step>
</Steps>
