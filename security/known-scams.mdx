Criminals regularly send fraudulent emails impersonating web hosting companies in an attempt to steal login credentials, payment information, or personal data. These emails are designed to look authentic and create a sense of urgency — pressuring you to click a link or make a payment before you have time to think carefully. Netstreet Africa maintains this page as a log of confirmed scam reports so you can identify and avoid them.

<Warning>
  **If you have already clicked a link or entered your credentials on a
  suspicious page:** Change your Netstreet Africa client area password
  immediately at [my.netstreet.co.za](https://my.netstreet.co.za), and contact
  our support team so we can check your account for unauthorised activity.
</Warning>

## How to tell if an email is really from Netstreet Africa

Before acting on any email that claims to be from Netstreet Africa, verify the following:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the sender's email address">
    Emails from Netstreet Africa will always come from an address ending in
    `@netstreet.co.za`. Check the actual **From** address in the email header —
    not just the display name, which can be faked. A display name that says
    "Netstreet Africa" but sends from `contact@some-other-domain.com` is a scam.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look for your account name and details">
    Legitimate emails from Netstreet Africa will always address you by name and
    may reference your specific account or domain. Generic greetings like
    **"Dear Customer"** or **"Dear User"** are a red flag.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hover over links before clicking">
    Before clicking any link in an email, hover your mouse pointer over it and
    look at the destination URL shown in the status bar or tooltip. It should
    point to `netstreet.co.za` — not a different domain, URL shortener, or
    lookalike address.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Never enter credentials via an email link">
    If you need to log in to your account, always type
    [my.netstreet.co.za](https://my.netstreet.co.za) directly into your browser
    rather than clicking a login link in an email.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check your actual renewal dates">
    Log in to your client area directly to verify when your services are actually
    due for renewal. Do not trust renewal notices in unsolicited emails.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  **Netstreet Africa will never ask for your password, credit card number, or
  other sensitive information directly via email.** Any request for sensitive
  information through email is a scam, regardless of how official it looks.
  All legitimate support communication happens within our support ticket system
  at [my.netstreet.co.za](https://my.netstreet.co.za).
</Info>

## What to do if you receive a suspicious email

1. **Do not click any links** in the email
2. **Do not reply** to the sender
3. **Do not enter any information** on any page linked from the email
4. **Move the email to your spam/junk folder** or delete it
5. **Contact Netstreet Africa support** at [my.netstreet.co.za](https://my.netstreet.co.za) if you are unsure whether the email is legitimate

## General phishing warning signs

Regardless of whether a specific scam is listed above, here are the most common warning signs of a phishing email:

| Warning sign | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Generic greeting ("Dear Customer") | Legitimate emails from Netstreet Africa address you by name |
| Urgent or threatening language | Scammers create panic to stop you thinking critically |
| Sender address does not match the displayed name | The "From" display name is easily faked |
| Links point to domains other than `netstreet.co.za` | Hover over links to see the real destination |
| Requests for password, card number, or personal info | Netstreet Africa never requests this via email |
| Poor spelling or grammar | Though many scams are now well-written, errors remain a warning sign |
| Unexpected attachments | Do not open attachments from unexpected or unverified senders |
