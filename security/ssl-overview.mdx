If you have ever noticed the padlock icon in your browser's address bar, you have already seen SSL in action. SSL (Secure Sockets Layer) — and its modern successor, TLS (Transport Layer Security) — are the security protocols that protect the connection between your website and its visitors. Every Netstreet Africa hosting account supports SSL, and installing a certificate on your site is one of the most important steps you can take to protect your visitors and your reputation. This page explains what SSL/TLS is, why it matters, and which type of certificate is right for your needs.

---

## What SSL/TLS actually does

SSL/TLS provides two critical security functions:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Encryption" icon="lock">
    All data transmitted between your web server and a visitor's browser is
    encrypted. This means that even if someone intercepts the traffic, they
    cannot read it. This is essential for any site that accepts login
    credentials, form submissions, or payment information.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Authentication" icon="badge-check">
    An SSL certificate proves to visitors that your site is genuinely operated
    by you — not an impersonator. Certificates are issued by trusted Certificate
    Authorities (CAs) that have verified your ownership of the domain (and, for
    higher-level certificates, your organisation's identity).
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

When a visitor connects to your site over HTTPS (`https://`), their browser and your server perform an **SSL/TLS handshake** — exchanging keys and verifying the certificate — before any data is transmitted. This happens transparently in a fraction of a second.

## Why your website needs an SSL certificate

<Warning>
  Modern web browsers such as Chrome and Firefox actively **warn visitors** when
  they connect to a site without SSL. A "Not Secure" warning in the address bar
  damages visitor trust and can significantly reduce conversions and traffic.
</Warning>

You need an SSL certificate if your site:

- Accepts **login credentials** (username and password)
- Processes **payments** or handles financial information
- Collects **personal data** via forms (names, addresses, phone numbers, etc.)
- Sends or receives **sensitive business information**

Beyond security, SSL also has practical benefits:

- **SEO advantage** — Google uses HTTPS as a ranking signal. Sites with SSL rank slightly higher than equivalent sites without it.
- **Browser compatibility** — Some browser features (service workers, geolocation, web push notifications) only work over HTTPS.
- **POPIA compliance** — South Africa's Protection of Personal Information Act requires appropriate technical measures to protect personal data in transit.

## Types of SSL certificates

Not all SSL certificates are equal. They differ in how thoroughly the issuing Certificate Authority verifies your identity, and in what they protect.

### By validation level

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Domain Validation (DV)">
    **Domain Validation (DV)** certificates are the most basic type. The Certificate Authority verifies only that you control the domain — no identity verification of you or your organisation takes place.

    - **Issued in:** Minutes to hours
    - **Best for:** Personal sites, blogs, development environments, small business sites
    - **Displays:** Padlock icon in the browser
    - **Example providers:** Let's Encrypt (free), Sectigo, Comodo

    <Note>
      **Let's Encrypt certificates** are DV certificates and are completely
      free. Netstreet Africa automatically provisions Let's Encrypt (or cPanel
      SSL) certificates for all new hosting accounts.
    </Note>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Organisation Validation (OV)">
    **Organisation Validation (OV)** certificates require the Certificate Authority to verify your organisation's legal identity, physical address, and phone number before issuing the certificate.

    - **Issued in:** 1–3 business days
    - **Best for:** Business websites, intranets, portals where organisational trust matters
    - **Displays:** Padlock + verified organisation details visible in certificate info
    - **Example providers:** DigiCert, Sectigo, GlobalSign
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Extended Validation (EV)">
    **Extended Validation (EV)** certificates involve the most rigorous identity verification process, including legal existence verification, physical address confirmation, and phone verification.

    - **Issued in:** 1–5 business days
    - **Best for:** Banks, large eCommerce sites, government portals, high-value transactions
    - **Displays:** Padlock + organisation name prominently displayed (in some browsers)
    - **Example providers:** DigiCert, Entrust, GlobalSign
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### By coverage

| Certificate type | What it protects |
|---|---|
| **Single domain** | One domain only (e.g. `yourdomain.co.za`) |
| **Wildcard** | One domain and all its subdomains (e.g. `*.yourdomain.co.za`) |
| **Multi-domain (SAN)** | Multiple different domains in a single certificate |

## Let's Encrypt vs paid certificates

Netstreet Africa automatically provisions **free SSL certificates** for all new hosting accounts. Here is how free and paid certificates compare:

| Feature | Let's Encrypt / cPanel SSL (Free) | Paid SSL Certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free | From ~R300/year |
| Validation type | Domain Validation (DV) | DV, OV, or EV |
| Browser trust | ✅ Fully trusted | ✅ Fully trusted |
| Auto-renewal | ✅ Automatic (every 90 days) | ❌ Manual renewal required |
| Wildcard support | ✅ Available | ✅ Available |
| Organisation name in cert | ❌ | ✅ (OV/EV only) |
| Warranty | ❌ No warranty | ✅ Included (varies by CA) |
| Site seal | ❌ | ✅ Static or dynamic seal |
| Best for | Most websites | eCommerce, banks, high-trust sites |

<Tip>
  For the vast majority of websites — including business sites, blogs, and
  small eCommerce stores — a free Let's Encrypt or cPanel SSL certificate
  provides exactly the same encryption strength as a paid certificate. The
  primary reasons to buy a paid certificate are if you need OV/EV validation,
  a warranty, or a dynamic site seal.
</Tip>

## Self-signed certificates

A self-signed certificate provides encryption but **no authentication** — it is signed by you rather than by a trusted Certificate Authority. Browsers will display a security warning when visitors try to access a site using a self-signed certificate, which will deter most users.

Self-signed certificates are only appropriate for:
- Internal development environments
- Testing before obtaining a real certificate
- Internal servers not accessed by the public

<Warning>
  Never use a self-signed certificate on a production website. The browser
  warnings they generate will erode visitor trust and may cause most users to
  leave your site immediately.
</Warning>
